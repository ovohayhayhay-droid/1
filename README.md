## Ensure access to skilled workforce

Domestic operations need a reliable pipeline of trained workers. To build that pipeline, partner with workforce boards, local agencies, and community colleges to recruit, train, and retain employees in the communities where operations will grow.

## Workforce partnership plan

- Work with regional workforce development boards to identify candidate pools and align hiring goals with local labor-market needs.
- Coordinate with local agencies and community-based organizations to reach job seekers who can benefit from stable domestic employment.
- Partner with community colleges and technical programs to build training pathways for role-specific skills, certifications, and apprenticeships.
- Offer paid training, work-based learning, and clear advancement paths so recruits can transition into long-term employment.

## TCO and onshoring analysis

- Compare offshore and onshore operating models using total cost of ownership rather than unit price alone.
- Include labor, freight, inventory carrying costs, training, quality issues, lead-time variability, and disruption risk in the analysis.
- Quantify long-term benefits of onshoring such as faster replenishment, lower supply interruption exposure, improved quality control, and stronger collaboration with domestic talent pipelines.
- Revisit the TCO model as hiring, supplier, and logistics assumptions change so investment decisions stay grounded in current conditions.

## Recommended U.S. operating locations

- **Columbus, Ohio:** strong logistics access, broad manufacturing labor pool, and reach into Midwest and East Coast demand centers.
- **Greenville-Spartanburg, South Carolina:** established advanced-manufacturing base, technical college partnerships, and access to southeastern freight corridors.
- **Dallas-Fort Worth, Texas:** major transportation hub, deep supplier ecosystem, and scale advantages for distribution and business services.
- **Indianapolis, Indiana:** central shipping position, steady operating costs, and access to skilled labor across warehousing, production, and maintenance roles.

Use these locations as the initial shortlist, then rank them on workforce availability, total cost of ownership, supplier proximity, freight performance, incentives, and resilience.

## Implementation priorities

1. Compare the shortlisted U.S. locations on workforce depth, supplier access, freight performance, and incentives.
2. Identify target operating regions and map the workforce boards, agencies, and colleges serving each area.
3. Define the core skills required for priority roles and share them with training partners.
4. Build a TCO model that compares onshore and offshore scenarios over a multi-year horizon.
5. Launch joint recruiting and training cohorts tied to actual domestic hiring demand.
6. Track outcomes such as applicant volume, training completion, placement rates, retention, and realized TCO improvements.
